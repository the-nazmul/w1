# Targeted state killings in Bangladesh during Hasina-era

This mini-project draws on the dataset behind Netra News's *Body Count* investigation (2023). It focuses on people killed in targeted, extrajudicial executions—distinct from protest-related shootings or deaths in custody—and asks how many of those victims were identified as having a political affiliation or an alleged link to extremist groups.

## Key points

- From 2009 through April 2022, Netra News identified roughly 2,600 deaths involving law enforcement across three categories: targeted killings, lethal force during protests, and custodial torture.
- About 2,000 were classified as "crossfire" or other premeditated, targeted killings; among that subset, 269 victims (this dataset) were affiliated with a political party or an armed group.
- Alleged leftist extremists — underground armed outfits such as the Purba Bangla Sarbahara Party, distinct from the mainstream legal left (e.g. the Communist Party of Bangladesh) — are the single largest group by far: 163 of 269, more than the alleged jihadists (26: JMB and similar) and the entire registered opposition (67: BNP and Jamaat-e-Islami) combined.
- A fourth group of 13 comes from the Chittagong Hill Tracts (UPDF, PCJSS/JSS) — regional parties, self-described leftist but primarily CHT-based, and killed mostly by the army rather than police/RAB. Added from the same Body Count data, where the source keeps them in their own category.
- Targeted killings matter specifically because "targeted" implies premeditation, and so a higher degree of state complicity than deaths during protests (which are not in this dataset and would likely fall more heavily on the political opposition).
